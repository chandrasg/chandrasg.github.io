Sharath Chandra Guntuku directs the [Computational Social Listening Lab](https://csl-lab-upenn.github.io/) at Penn and is Secondary Faculty at the [Annenberg School for Communication](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/).

His research examines how people **express psychological states, make health decisions, and respond to interventions**, using computational methods to study these questions at scale. He develops **AI (ML/NLP) approaches** to measure constructs such as mental health, loneliness, and cultural norms from digital trace data, including social media, electronic health records, and online reviews.

A central focus is understanding how these patterns **vary across populations, by geography and culture**. This includes work on algorithmic fairness in mental health assessment and cross-cultural studies of language and behavior spanning the U.S., China, India, and sub-Saharan Africa. He also designs and deploys **conversational agents for health behavior change**, translating behavioral insights into scalable interventions.

This interdisciplinary agenda, spanning computer science, communication, and behavioral science, is supported by over **$5.6 million USD** in external funding and has led to publications in *PNAS*, *JAMA Network Open*, *npj Digital Medicine*, *ACM CHI*, *ACL/EMNLP/NAACL (\*CL)*, and *AAAI ICWSM*, with implementation partners including state Departments of Health and the World Bank Group.

His work has been covered by [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/ai-chatbots-treatment-mental-health), [Thomson Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/ai-fails-detect-depression-signs-social-media-posts-by-black-americans-study-2024-03-28/), [Politico](https://www.politico.com/newsletters/future-pulse/2024/03/27/ais-not-ready-for-depression-diagnoses-00149257), [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/an-ai-used-facebook-data-to-predict-mental-illness/), [The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/health/archive/2019/11/what-your-tweets-say-about-your-mood/601510/), [The Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/health/drug-side-effects-penn-research-analysis-ai-social-media-20260504.html), and other [media venues](/media/).
