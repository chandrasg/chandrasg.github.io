Keep the denominator visible

October concerned authorization for ages 5–11; February concerned ages 6 months–4. February includes 27,107 records labeled “feb” and 2,267 labeled “postponed” in the released dataset.

These are post-deduplication counts. The original 173,109 comments and removed duplicate multiplicities are not available in the release, so this chart cannot show original submission volume or duplication rates. No theme prevalence by date can be recovered without the document–topic matrix.

What reproduced—and what remains unresolved

  • All 13 theme totals and 39 printed example-topic memberships agree with the release.
  • Table 2 labels one word list T21; the released list belongs to T24, as correctly printed in Table 3.
  • The paper reports five positive/neutral selected examples. Only four cells match the workbook legend color; two other cells use an unexplained color. Their labels cannot be resolved automatically.

The workbook contains 1,000 selected topic-example slots but 994 unique example texts. The reported 0.5% concerns those selected slots; it is not a full-corpus or population opinion estimate. This companion does not infer stance for the 149,897 comments.

Method and release limits

The published analysis selected 100 topics from eight LDA candidates. This companion recounts the released annotations; it does not retrain LDA, reproduce human model selection, or calculate fuzzy-template similarity. The released notebook applies NLTK POS tagging and WordNet lemmatization despite the paper describing spaCy. Full candidate models, document–topic weights, training commands/seeds and removed duplicates were not found in the three-file OSF release.
