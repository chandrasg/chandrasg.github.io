Understanding vaccine narratives: the what, why, and how

What can nearly 150,000 public comments tell us about vaccine opposition? This study pairs AI topic modeling with human interpretation to examine the concerns, beliefs, and persuasive strategies in comments about authorizing COVID-19 vaccines for children.

Read the research paper ↗

The central insight: a vaccine argument has more than a factual claim. The study also identifies the worldview beneath it and the way it is made persuasive—a useful framework for listening to audiences and designing health communication.

How to read these counts: each bar counts model topics, not people or comments. The themes overlap, so the bars cannot be added to estimate prevalence. Labels such as “Unsafe” describe commenters’ arguments; they are not findings about vaccine effects.

Why this matters for health communication

The paper argues that responding to a specific concern is only one part of the task. Communication also needs to engage with the trust, values, and rhetorical strategies shaping how a message is received.

These communication implications come from a descriptive analysis of public comments.

How the study was done

From comments to a framework

The authors analyzed comments submitted to two FDA regulatory dockets in October 2021 and February 2022, concerning vaccine authorization for ages 5–11 and 6 months–4 years. After removing exact duplicates, they used topic modeling to group recurring language, then interpreted those groups through collaborative thematic and discourse analysis.

What the study can tell us

This is a study of discourse in self-selected public comments during two specific periods. It helps characterize vaccine opposition; it does not estimate how common these views were among parents or the general public.
