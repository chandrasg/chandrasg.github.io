PEDIATRIC VACCINE COMMENTS: HISTORICAL RESEARCH COMPANION

Source study: Golos, Aleksandra M.; Guntuku, Sharath-Chandra; Buttenheim, Alison M. (2024). “Do not inject our babies”: a social listening analysis of public opinion about authorizing pediatric COVID-19 vaccines. Health Affairs Scholar 2(7), qxae082.
Paper: https://doi.org/10.1093/haschl/qxae082
Original public data and methods: https://osf.io/r7ewf/

WHAT THIS PAGE SHOWS

The interactive page recounts the authors’ released annotations and aggregate comment totals. It covers October 2021 and February 2022, and is a historical research resource. It is a partial public-release reproduction, not a new public-opinion survey or a live health-advice tool.

The 149,897 released rows have distinct record IDs and processed messages, including one empty processed message. There are 120,523 October records, 27,107 labeled “feb,” and 2,267 labeled “postponed.” The daily and period totals describe retained comments after exact deduplication. October concerned authorization for ages 5–11; February concerned ages 6 months–4.

All 13 published theme counts agree with Tables 1–3. Five of the 100 topics are marked low quality; the other 95 have 177 total theme assignments. The three domains cover 52, 47 and 47 distinct topics, respectively. Themes overlap: a topic can appear in several bars. These counts cannot be interpreted as percentages of comments or people.

The page supports domain selection, theme-overlap comparisons, inspection of all 100 topic assignments, two comment-date windows and aggregate CSV download. It embeds no individual comments, selected example texts, commenter identifiers, respondent metadata, or model top-word lists. Topic numbers identify model topics only. Theme labels describe commenters’ arguments as coded in the study, not evidence of vaccine effects.

WHAT WAS REPRODUCED

The companion uses three original OSF version-1 files released in September 2023: the comments CSV, preprocessing HTML and topic/theme mapping workbook. Their byte lengths and SHA256 hashes were checked before analysis. The historical source notebook was inspected but not executed.

Of 64 selected published targets, 62 agree: all 13 theme counts, all 39 selected example-topic memberships, nine of ten narrative counts, and one of two complete word-list identity checks. These are related target comparisons, not 64 independent statistical tests. The membership checks concern topic IDs, not verification of every example text or word list. See validation.txt for the two retained discrepancies and independent checks.

The page does not retrain LDA, regenerate model outputs, repeat human topic selection or adjudication, or infer stance across the comment corpus.

INTERPRETATION LIMITS

The corpus consists of self-selected comments and is not representative of population opinion. The release is already deduplicated. The original 173,109 comments, 23,212 removed records, 4,890 repeated texts and template repeated 647 times cannot be independently recounted. No theme prevalence by date can be recovered without the document–topic matrix.

The source preprocessing HTML applies NLTK tokenization, POS tagging and WordNet lemmatization, while the paper describes spaCy. The original raw inputs, complete model-specific processing, pinned NLP resources, LDA checkpoints, candidate outputs, seeds and training commands are unavailable. The original 100-topic fit, eight-model selection and representative-comment selection are not regenerated.

The release provides no exact reference-template selection or fuzzy-similarity implementation. The reported 0.80% at similarity at least 0.5 and the associated supplement plots are not reproduced.

The workbook has 1,000 selected topic-example slots containing 994 distinct texts. The reported 0.5% neutral/positive finding concerns those selected slots, not the whole corpus or population. Four cells match the workbook’s neutral/positive legend color; the paper reports five, and two further cells have an unexplained color. No new stance labels are assigned.

Some source-local indices recur between period labels. The analysis uses the unique released record identifiers for counting, without treating them as unique people; no identifiers are published here. The empty processed message is retained to preserve the release’s denominator; its handling in the original LDA fit is unknown.
